## Slide 88

- As a practical matter
- We often make enough assumptions to know we’ll find an interior solution. Then use Lagrange with equality constraints.
- If you’re not sure this is the case you’ll have to approach problem more carefully
  - Specify different possible cases
    - Use economic logic to identify and focus on most likely cases
    - When constraints don’t bind they can be ignored. Solve using remaining equalities (FOC and/or binding constraints).
    - Compare objective value at different candidate solutions to find the best.
  - Alternative method of slack variables (described in book).

## Slide 98

- Solving problems with multiple constraints
- Work out solution for each possible constraint set.
  - i.e. separately solve under assumptions:
    - >0, =0
    - =0, >0
    - >0, >0
    - You get a solution from each, but not all feasible (e.g. setting a multiplier to zero ignores that constraint, but solution you then get may in fact violate that constraint, and is hence infeasible).
  - Choose feasible candidate solution that gives highest value.
