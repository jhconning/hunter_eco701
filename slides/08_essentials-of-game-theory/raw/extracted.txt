## Slide 10

- What are the questions?
- How do individuals and societies allocate resources?
  - Far broader than study of markets.
  - how agents (individuals, households, firms, groups) might choose to associate and contract and the rules of the game they play by 
  - strategies, contracts, organizations, property rights, institutions and policy.
  - Outcomes: Conflict or cooperation.  Efficient, equitable?
  - Mechanism design: getting incentives right to get better outcomes.
- Methods and Assumptions
    - Methodological individualism  and instrumental rationality
    - optimization subject to constraints and marginal analysis
  - Feedback, interactions, and equilibrium.
  - Ceteribus paribus and comparative statics
- Challenges on behavioral and other grounds

- Player 2 (column)
- Player 1(row)
-  This game has no equilibrium in dominant strategies.  
-  Arrows indicate each player’s best responses. 
-  A Nash Equilibrium in pure strategies is found where horizontal and vertical arrows point to the same cell.

## Slide 16

- Nash Equilibria
-  Existence: A Nash Equilibrium in pure strategies may not exist.  However, every game has at least one Nash equilibrium (possibly in mixed strategies).
- Uniqueness:  Many games have many multiple Nash equilibria.
- More on mixed strategies later..

## Slide 17

![Image](media/slide017_pic0001.png)
- John Nash’s (1950) PhD dissertation  (just 28 pages) defined a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium for any game with a finite set of actions. Proved at least one (mixed-strategy) Nash equilibrium must exist in such a game. 
- Cournot (1838) had used a version of the idea, but not stated in general form.
- John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern Theory of Games and Economic Behavior 
- Had not been able to frame the problem to pin down useful equilibrium concepts in general games (beyond obvious ones like in dominant strategies). Minimax theorem gave Nash eqn but only works for zero-sum games.

## Slide 19

![Image](media/slide019_pic0004.png)
- Fisherman’s Dilemma
- F12 is a dominant strategy (F10 is a ‘dominated strategy’): F12 gives a higher payoff, regardless of what opponent does.
- This game has an equilibrium in dominant strategies (F12, F12). 
- It is an inefficient outcome --  (F10, F10) Pareto dominates:   both could be made better off.
